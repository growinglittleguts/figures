The infant gut is an immune training ground

It is an immune organ that happens to digest food. The gut bacteria that settle in during the first months of life — your baby's gut garden — are training an immune system that will protect them for decades. Those signals reach nearly every organ in the body.

~70% of immune cells live in the gut
~80% of antibody-producing cells start here
Oral tolerance is not a default in early life
Bifidobacterium Bacteroides Other bacteria Immune cells (GALT)

Breast milk is active immune education, not passive nutrition

Breast milk shapes the gut microbiota, delivers immune cells that persist via microchimerism, and transfers antigens that can induce either tolerance or priming — depending on dose, timing, and maternal immune status (Verhasselt, 2024).
